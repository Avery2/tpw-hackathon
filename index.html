Transformative Programming Works

A unified voice for the community organizations offering programs in California state prisons.

The Failing System

  • 95% of California's incarcerated citizens will reenter society
  • 44% will be reincarcerated within 3 years of release
  • Only 1 of 31 California prisons is fully served today

A Model That Works

  • Community-based organizations (CBOs) reduce reincarceration to 21%
  • Led by people from the communities most affected by incarceration
  • Lifelines to the community upon reentry

What We Can't Prove Yet

  • Every field TPW collects describes activity: who enrolled, how long a class ran
  • None of it is joined to parole or recidivism records
  • So the 21% figure comes from research, not from TPW's own programs
What is a CBO?

The gap between activity and impact

CDCR spends under one percent of its budget on rehabilitation. TPW advocates across 120 organizations — but its reporting stops at process data. The outcome records exist. They have never been joined.

What we're building

A framework that keeps CBO data entry simple, then makes the outcomes joinable — so TPW can report impact from its own programs, not just cite research about someone else's.

01

Collect

Keep the form CBOs already use. One row per program cycle, one row per yard — add the identifiers needed to link a cohort to a release. Working intake form →

02

Join

Match cohorts to parole and recidivism records at the facility-period level, without moving individual identifiers out of CDCR custody.

03

Prove

Return outcome rates per program, facility, and year — the evidence behind the 44% → 21% claim, sourced to TPW's own members.
